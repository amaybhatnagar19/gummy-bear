The landing page can include multiple sections without becoming multiple screens.

Suggested sections include Hero, How It Works, Features, Privacy, and FAQ.

The main CTA should consistently direct parents to the PWA.

### **Experience B: Gummy Bear PWA**

This is where parents actually use Gummy Bear.

The PWA would contain the product’s functional experience, including:

- Parent onboarding
- Child profiles
- Toy inventory
- Camera-based toy addition
- Toy organization
- Age-appropriate activity suggestions
- Toy usage tracking
- Toy status management
- Reuse and donation guidance

The landing page and PWA would share the same visual identity.

However, the PWA would prioritize usability over decoration.

For example, we might use cute 3D illustrations during onboarding but simpler icons and cards in the inventory.

That creates visual continuity without making everyday tasks unnecessarily complicated.

**One main action does not mean only one available action.** A screen can support secondary actions as long as the primary next step is obvious.

**Screen to be merged →**

There are four opportunities to simplify the experience.

| **Screens or steps** | **Recommendation** | **Reason** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome + Add First Toy | Merge | Both introduce the first interaction |
| Child Age + Full Child Profile | Separate by timing | Ask age before ideas; full profile after signup |
| Toy Status + Toy Details | Merge | Status editing belongs in toy management |
| Install Prompt + Home | Merge | Installation can be a contextual Home prompt |

Avoid building separate screens for Quick Play, Creative Adventure, and Play Together.

These are **activity categories**, not destinations.

All three should appear together in the Activity Suggestions screen.

## 14. Principles

### **1. Playful by Nature, Thoughtful by Design**

Gummy Bear should feel like a delightful, playful world built around childhood, without becoming visually overwhelming or childish for parents. Soft pastel colors, rounded shapes, friendly 3D illustrations, cute characters, and playful typography should create warmth while maintaining clarity and usability.

### **2. Speak Like a Helpful Friend, Not a Software Product**

Every headline, description, button, and instruction should use simple, warm, conversational language that parents immediately understand. Focus on what parents can accomplish rather than technical features. Avoid jargon, exaggerated marketing claims, and unnecessarily complicated explanations.

### **3. Make Every Interaction Feel Effortless**

Every screen should have an obvious purpose, a clear next action, and as little friction as possible. Parents should never have to figure out what to do next. Use progressive disclosure, intuitive navigation, and short, meaningful interactions rather than overwhelming users with options or information.

### **4. One Consistent World Across Website and PWA**

The public landing website and Gummy Bear PWA should feel like two parts of the same product, not separate experiences. Maintain consistent colors, typography, illustration styles, components, tone of voice, and interaction patterns. The transition from discovering Gummy Bear to using it should feel natural and seamless.

### **5. Build Trust Through Calm, Honest Design**

Gummy Bear should feel safe, reassuring, and respectful of parents’ decisions. Communicate privacy, recommendations, and product limitations clearly without creating anxiety. Give parents visible control over their children’s information and toys. Use thoughtful explanations rather than pressure, guilt, or manipulative messaging.

---

### **One overarching design philosophy**

**Gummy Bear should look playful enough to celebrate childhood, feel simple enough for busy parents, and communicate thoughtfully enough to earn their trust.**
