## 1. The job

When My child feels bored with the existing set of toys, I want to have creative ideas, game hacks and mini-missions to extend playtime and make every toy more engaging, so I can remove the boredom of my kid with the existing toys while not have to purchase new toys everytime this happens.

Who, by situation (not age, city or title): 

A parent of a child between the age of 2 to 8 years generally having their space cluttered with a lot of toys coming in through gifts by others, or they buying themselves whenever they feel their child is outgrowing their toys or are bored with the existing one’s. Also, another scenarios is their child plays with a specific set of toys only. Because of all this their home becomes cluttered and the old toys are ignored. They actually want to find, personalize, and extend the joy of toys—by blending discovery, emotional intelligence, and playful delight.

Today they hire: They generally (Scrap them) dump these toys in garbage or give those to someone without thinking if they need it or not. Sometimes they sell them on platforms that gives an option for selling 2nd hand toys. But ultimately they end up feeling like they overspent on toys.

## 2. The switch

What they'd fire: 

1. The option of Scraping or dumping these toys in garbage.
2. Selling the toys on the platforms that gives an option for selling 2nd hand toys
3. Continue to keep the toys in a storage space in their house and making the space cluttered.
4. Chat GPT (Free Plan) and Google Search
5. Discussing things or taking guidance with other parents with child in similar age groups or taking generic advice from elders in the household

The forces that matter:
Push, outside them (what's going wrong in their life right now): 

1. Unable to use the existing set of toys which creates boredom for the kid and the need to buy newer toys again and again within a very short duration
2. Not being able to utilise the existing set of toys to their full potential which leads to them being kept in a storage space without being used.

Pull, inside them (what they've always wanted): 

Give the best from the available options to the child and become a parent who helps shape their child’s future and their overall personality.

Anxiety (the risk of trying you: what they tried before, or a decision that's hard to undo):

1. Anxiety of not following the standard or defined path set by the society standards, leads to thinking if this does not work then the child’s development will be in jeopardy.
2. It won’t know about the development and growth of Indian Kids and how they use their toys.
3. The output generated can be repetitive and generic for all
4. Depriving the kid/child from the newer toys
5. Worrying about sharing the photos or gallery app full access to the app.

Habit (the way they already do it):

1. Immediately buying the toys once the child starts to feel bored with the existing set and moving the existing set into the storage space
2. As soon as the new toy comes, immediately giving it to the kid, moving their attention from the older one’s.
3. If the toy outgrows the child’s age, then moving it into the storage space instead of donating.

What the product does about each: [force] -> When a parent until they get the first value from the app, only shares the limited access to specific set of photos or just clicks the images from the inbuilt camera option this shall help them remove this worry.

The one worry onboarding must remove: 

1. The output generated can be repetitive and generic for all
2. Depriving the kid/child from the newer toys

## 3. The core flow

Today (what they do now, before your product):

Flow 1 → 

1. Child Gets a new toy
2. Old toy gets moved into storage space
3. Child gets bored with the toy in a few days and demands for a new toy
4. Parent thinks of reusing it in a different way
5. Buys a new toy instead
6. The previous gets moved to the storage space and the clutter builds up

Flow 2 →

1. Child gets stuck playing with a single toy
2. Parent thinks of going back to few of the older toys to reuse them with creative ideas
3. Does not find any ideas
4. Leaves the idea of revisiting the old toys
5. Let the child play with the existing toy as they feel engaged

Flow 3 →

1. The child outgrows the toys
2. The parent think of reusing the older toys for reusing them in the household with some out of the box ideas or donating them to others who will be able to use them.
3. Does not find a suitable idea or people who they can donate it to.
4. Keeps the toys back in the storage or scrap them instead.

Flow 4 → 

1. The parent has constant thought of fomo or delima of being an efficient & understanding parent
2. Goes to some of the other parents in their friend circle or seek guidance from the elders from their house
3. Gets generic advice or the advice from their experiences which does not solve their inner anxiety or thought
4. Continues to worry or get the same thought and ends up overspending on things which sometimes are not needed for the kids.

Flow 5 → 

1. The parent thinks “How can my behaviour and habits affect/shape my child’s growth and the overall personality” seeking more personalised answer.
2. They either go to Google search or Chat GPT (free tool) to ask those questions without the all the personal context.
3. Gets the standard answers based on the available information online.
4. Does not find the answers helpful
5. Continues to think and take actions as per the friends and families suggestions or as per the answers shown on Google or Chat GPT.

Flow 6 → 

1. Parent wants to find personalised toys for their kids but available options in the market are mostly segmented with Gender bracket like a Car for 2-5 years old boy or a Doll for for 3-4 years of Girl. And then there are some toys which focuses on the overall development of child and are grouped age wise but then they are also generic and is one for all option. 
2. Parent does not find personalised options available.
3. Ends up buying the generic options which are built with the concept of 1 product for all.
